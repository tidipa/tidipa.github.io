## Summary

At Pāvā the Mallas have built a new assembly hall, Ubbhataka, and the Bhagavā is the first to use it; after teaching late into the night he lies down and asks āyasmā Sāriputta to speak to the bhikkhū. Sāriputta tells of the quarrels among the Jains after the death of Nigaṇṭha Nāṭaputta and proposes that the bhikkhū recite the Dhamma together and not quarrel, so that the optimal life may last long; he then recites it arranged by number, from one to ten. The Bhagavā rises and approves.

## Commentary

This is a proto-Abhidhamma sutta that provides lists of related (and opposing) terms grouped by ascending numerical order (by size of list). It represents a largely Theravādin view of the core elements of the Buddha's teaching. The corresponding version in the Dharmaguptaka Dīrghāgama — DA11 (Discourse Increasing by One) — is much shorter and better structured. The intention of the list is for communal recitation across the fourfold assembly of monastics and lay disciples.

Although some of the lists are probably derived from the Buddha's teachings, it seems some lists have been inserted at a later stage by disciples. Given these lists are presented outside the context of the Buddha's teachings, it is sometimes hard to understand the purpose and meaning for the items.
